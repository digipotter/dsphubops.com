Peak season does not wait for a slow hiring process. If a driver needs a background check, a drug test, paperwork and a training slot before their first route, the real deadline for posting the ad is weeks before the date you need them.

The fix is to work backwards. Pick the date you need new drivers running routes, then schedule each step in reverse.

Start with the number and the date

Decide how many additional drivers you need and by what date. Then pad the number. Some candidates will not return calls, some will fail a check, and some will take another job. Look at your last hiring push to see how many offers it took to get one driver on the road, and plan for at least that ratio again.

Also line up a few extra training slots and spare vans. A late hire is better than no hire, but only if you have room to train them and a route to put them on.

Set screening criteria before you post

Write your must-haves down so every screener applies the same standard. A typical list:

  • Minimum age and license requirements, set by your insurer and any delivery partner rules.
  • Driving record standards your insurer accepts.
  • Ability to meet the physical demands of the job.
  • Availability for the full peak period, including weekends and holidays.
  • Work authorization and a willingness to complete the required checks.

Background check and screening rules differ by state and sometimes by city, including how and when you can consider a candidate's history. Confirm your process with an employment attorney, and follow the screening requirements your insurer and delivery partner set.

A week-by-week plan

The table below is an example plan counted back from the first day new drivers run routes. Your lead times will differ, so replace the week numbers with what your own vendors and training schedule actually take.

Weeks before start Focus What to finish
10 to 9 Plan and post Headcount target, screening criteria, ad with honest route and pay details, first postings live
8 to 7 Screen Phone screens within one business day of each application, interview slots booked
7 to 6 Interview In-person or video interviews, conditional offers sent, backup list started
6 to 5 Checks Background checks and drug tests started right after acceptance
5 to 3 Paperwork Onboarding forms, results received, training dates confirmed with each hire
3 to 2 Train Classroom and ride-along training, uniforms and device setup
1 Confirm Final reminders, route and buddy assignments, first-day instructions sent
0 Start New drivers run routes with a named buddy or dispatcher check-in

Keep the process moving

Speed matters more than polish. A good candidate who waits three days for a callback can take another offer. Set a target, such as responding to every applicant within one business day, and assign a person to own it.

Book interview slots in blocks, for example two mornings a week, so you are not rebuilding your calendar for each candidate. Offer a few time options and make rescheduling easy.

Start background checks and drug tests as soon as a candidate accepts. These steps often take the longest and depend on outside vendors and clinic availability, so any delay here pushes the whole timeline.

Keep candidates warm until day one

The gap between acceptance and the first day is where many new hires disappear. Fill it with contact:

  • Send a confirmation the same day they accept, with the next three steps and dates.
  • Text or email a reminder two days before every appointment, test or training session.
  • Check in once a week with a short message: "Everything on track? Questions?"
  • Send first-day details a few days ahead: where to park, what to bring, who to ask for, what time.
  • Keep a backup list of qualified candidates you can call when someone drops out.

Track every candidate in one shared sheet with the stage, the date of the last contact and the next step. If a name has no next step, someone needs to call.

Review it after peak

Once the season ends, note where candidates dropped off: after the ad, before the interview, during checks, or between acceptance and day one. That tells you where to spend effort next year.
